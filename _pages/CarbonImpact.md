Here, I try to keep track of the carbon impact caused by my job as a researcher. **Why?** First of all, I think it is important that we are all aware that everyone is contributing to carbon emissions and consequentially climate change. Currently, we are drastically failing to follow the goals set by the so-called '1.5°C goal' according to the <a href='https://unfccc.int/process-and-meetings/the-paris-agreement'>Paris agreement.</a>. Second, due to my current funding through the MSCA postdoctoral fellowhsip with the project <a href='https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101208259'>QNETS</a>, I obliged myself to follow the <a href='https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/priorities-2019-2024/european-green-deal_en'> European green deal agenda</a> and I promised in particular to keep track of the CO2 emissions caused by traveling for my project. Finally, I saw Marc-Olivier Renou doing this on his <a href='https://marcolivierrenou.com/carbon-impact/'> website</a> and I am stealing this good habit from him.


**The importance of traveling in Academia**

Nowadays, traveling takes on an essential role in research. Partially because it is necessary to participate in international conferences, to visit 
other research groups, and to attend committee meetings. Whether we like it or not, these things are typically asked when we apply to a position or for funding and a 'good research CV' includes typically some decend amount of traveling. However, certainly we (at least I do) also travel because it gives great pleasure to exchange about research in person and of course also to discover new places. While the Covid pandemic showed that online meetings, coordination via messanger tools, and even onlince conference can work, I have personally the impression that nothing is as efficient and as nice as discussing research in person.   

**Carbon impact of traveling**

To start with some transparancy about my carbon impact, I will keep track here of my carbon emissions caused by traveling for professional reasons. One day, once I understand enough of how to keep track of it, I might expand and include things like the CO2 cost of staying at hotels, buying office equipment, etc. I will also start by tracking only my long-distance travels and do not include, for instance, my daily way to work by bus. 

**2026**

* Seefeld, Austria for the <a href='https://ritschgroup.uibk.ac.at/seefeld2026/index.htm'> Seefeld conference.</a> TGV and ICE trains via Munich back and forth, and Flixbus between Munich and Seefeld back and forth ~850km ≈ 12.2 kg CO2e.


**Methodology**

I am still working on finding a reliable method to find out the CO2e (carbon dioxide equivalent) emissions for each journey. It's suprisingly hard to find accurate details on this and the various tools/calculators that one finds online give different results. For now, I try to go case-by-case. Ideally, I find the details on the website of whatever company I travelled with. Otherwise, I am using for now the information and the calculator provided by the <a href='https://agirpourlatransition.ademe.fr/particuliers/bureau/calculer-emissions-carbone-trajets'> French ecological transition agency.</a>
